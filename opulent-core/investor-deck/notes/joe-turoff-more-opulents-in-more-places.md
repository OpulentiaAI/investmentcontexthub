# More Opulents in more places

## Self-driving knowledge work

AI can do far more than most businesses have put it to work doing. Teams still leave valuable research unfinished, delay decisions, and limit how many customers they can serve because people have to coordinate every step.

Better models alone will not resolve that constraint. Companies need to change how they organize the work. We started Opulent so the people who understand a business can direct agents toward its most important problems.

Self-driving knowledge work means giving Opulent an outcome to achieve and the boundaries it must respect. Opulent determines the work required, coordinates agents across the company's systems, and checks progress against the goal. As conditions change, it should adapt its approach and improve the process itself.

We are building from the work Opulent runs today toward that operating model. The ambition is for teams to pursue more of what they know is possible, without having to plan and supervise every action themselves.

We are already seeing what becomes possible. A research institute's quarterly survey review improved more than 7x in accuracy against a held-out answer key. A law partner brought input from fifty stakeholders into a fully redlined document. A chief marketing officer runs an autonomous pipeline and CRM through email. In our own business, a prospect booking a meeting starts two runs. One prepares the client materials. The other provisions the compute we will serve them on.

About 23 engagements have come through referral and targeted outreach. Winter of Code Social put our research-preview coding agent in front of more than 2,000 engineers. Ramp selected Opulent as one of the first five companies to build on its Agent Wallet. We are design partners with Ramp, Stripe, and Mesa.

The product is changing what customers expect to get done. The lead of a private equity search fund told us Opulent 2 made him the most productive he had been in two years. An exited founder said it made him think bigger. Nearly everyone who received free tokens in the preview spent them all, at a pace we had not planned for.

That is the opportunity we are building for. As agents become more capable, teams can take on work they previously could not justify starting.

## Our predictions on the future of work

Our five predictions, adapted from [Thorsten Ball's propositions](https://x.com/thorstenball/status/2101305394190557466) for autonomous knowledge work:

- **Reviewing every piece of work will disappear.** People will judge the operation and its results while agents check individual documents, records, and actions.

- **Manual production will stop defining knowledge work.** Agents will produce the reports, research, and presentations while people choose the problems worth solving and decide what to do with the results.

- **Roles built around handoffs will disappear.** Agents will carry work from investigation through execution, removing the need for people whose main job is passing instructions and collecting updates.

- **Operating software by hand will disappear from routine work.** People will specify an outcome while agents navigate applications, move information, and complete the steps required to achieve it.

- **Fixed dashboards and menus will give way to interfaces built on demand.** Agents will assemble the evidence and controls a person needs for the decision in front of them.

## What Opulent can do today

That future begins with work a team can hand over today. Opulent starts from incoming events, investigates with connected tools, and coordinates agents across a process. The capabilities below address the practical reasons that work otherwise stops: cost, missing access, disconnected systems, and lost context.

### Fusion makes sustained work more economical

Running agents across a business changes the cost question. A process may require many investigations, several rounds of correction, and repeated runs. The useful measure is what it costs to produce a result the team accepts.

Fusion gives planning and review to a frontier lead agent, with a less expensive agent handling delegated execution. Each keeps its own context. The lead checks what comes back and takes over when the work needs more judgment. We tune the instructions for each pairing, including the detail in a brief and which work the lead delegates.

Cognition's published Fusion evaluation with Artificial Analysis and Vals AI illustrates the potential of this approach. Its recommended pairing reduced costs by 23 to 46 percent across five coding benchmarks while staying within two points of the frontier model on each. On the migration benchmark, it scored higher at 41 percent lower cost. These are Cognition's results on coding tasks, rather than measurements of Opulent's business workflows. [Source](https://cognition.com/blog/local-fusion)

### Voice lets people direct work wherever they are

A person can talk through a goal with Opulent, question its approach, and hand off the work during the conversation. The call stays in the session history, so execution can begin without a second brief.

When a run needs a decision, Opulent can call, message, or ask in the existing thread. The person can clarify a constraint and continue with their day while the agent works.

### Computer and browser use reach the work itself

Opulent operates web applications and desktop software. It identifies controls from the application's accessibility information or the screen, refuses actions based on a stale observation, and stops a batch when an action fails.

Our recorded verification executed 21 of 21 browser actions and 13 of 13 desktop actions. Those are bounded checks, not a general task-success rate. People can watch, take control, and review recordings. Physical input stops the agent; login and verification prompts go to the user.

### Machines on every major operating system expand what agents can own

Each agent gets its own machine on macOS, Linux, or Windows, so work can reach the accounting software or desktop application a business actually uses.

For a prospective manufacturer engagement, the software lives inside a private network. We are designing access around temporary machine identities and the customer's rules. The environment must fit the work before the agent can take responsibility for it.

### Payments let work continue within a budget

Ramp's Agent Wallet lets Opulent purchase a service needed for an assignment within a defined role and budget. Human checkpoints apply where required. Payment IDs, approval records, and receipts stay with the work, and a denied request does not widen the agent's authority.

### Scans make broad review practical

A sample of contracts cannot identify every renewal outside policy. An Opulent scan divides the collection among agents, using an agreed definition of a finding and the evidence needed to confirm it. Opulent validates the findings and brings the owner evidence and proposed changes.

A completed scan can become recurring work. Scheduled or event-triggered passes check new records, keeping the review current as the business changes.

### Auto-triage takes responsibility for the queue

An invoice-exceptions channel or client-request inbox needs attention as work arrives. A persistent Opulent monitor groups related messages into cases and starts an investigation with the tools allowed for that assignment.

The investigator returns the cause, action taken, or decision needed. The monitor retains ownership and recent case history; corrections can improve future routing. By the time the owner opens a case, the investigation can already be waiting.

### Dynamic workflows carry a process through multiple stages

A team can ask Opulent to review every vendor contract against a new template. Agents prepare redlines, flag clauses requiring counsel, and combine the findings into one review queue. Completed work is recorded so an interrupted run can resume.

For larger collections, a coordinator divides records among parallel agents and reconciles their findings. The research institute's quarterly survey review used this approach, improving accuracy more than 7x against a held-out answer key. A successful workflow can become a reusable skill, with changes checked against prior results.

### Swarms expand how much work a team can take on

Opulent assigns independent workstreams to agents on separate machines. A coordinator stays responsible for the combined result, resolves conflicts, and stops or redirects unproductive runs within the assigned budget.

A team could carry out a reporting migration across portfolio companies or investigate accounts in several regions at once. The person directs the assignment without becoming the messenger between every agent.

### Codemode reduces the overhead of repeated actions

When an assignment spans many records, Opulent can write a program to perform the repeated tool operations together. It loops through records, branches on results, and returns the information needed for the next decision. The team's permissions still apply.

### Drive keeps working context available across runs

Drive keeps files and procedures available to an organization, project, or person beyond an individual session. Version history preserves earlier work, and separate workspaces let agents explore changes without overwriting one another.

Our work with Mesa extends this to document review. A person can inspect changes, accept or reject them, and restore an earlier version.

### Memory turns feedback into better procedures

Opulent can review a session and propose improvements to the next run. A corrected routing decision becomes a better rule. A failed run reveals a missing check. A successful approach becomes a playbook with decision points and test tasks.

People can accept, edit, or reject proposed knowledge updates. The next scheduled run can begin with the context and corrections the previous one produced.

## An independent agent company

We are building Opulent independently of any one model lab. We can choose and combine models according to the work, then change that combination as better options become available.

The customer should continue working with one Opulent. The standard stays the same: complete the assignment within its constraints and return evidence the owner can check. A less expensive model is useful when it lowers the cost of accepted work. More capable models are useful when they solve harder problems or reduce the correction needed.

We expect both to improve. Our job is to turn those improvements into greater capacity for the businesses using Opulent.

## Building around the customer's work

Deploying an agent starts with understanding how a business operates. Its policies, systems, and review practices determine what an agent must know and when it should ask for help.

For a leadership search firm, a few meetings produced a new way to organize that context. The firm's ideal customer profile, buying signals, playbooks, and runbooks live as connected, typed files. They are available alongside the tools the firm already uses. The agent begins with the firm's working context, including which claims need evidence.

Opulent also found $20,000 in startup credits from E2B, an opportunity we might otherwise have missed.

We also use Opulent to build the systems behind these deployments. A few prompts produced a reinforcement learning stack based on Mercor's APEX-Agents recipe, SkyRL, and AIBuildAI's post-training agent. It gives us a way to prepare client work for training and evaluation. Accepted work can become evidence for improving the model or the procedure, with the change checked before it is used more broadly.

We are rewriting computer use natively in Rust because observation and action repeat throughout a desktop task. Reducing that overhead can improve the time needed to complete the whole process.

Each deployment helps us understand what the product needs next. The difficult work is often specific to the business: what counts as a correct answer, who owns an exception, and which changes require approval. Those details belong in the system if the agent is going to operate reliably over time.

## What the future looks like

A recent experiment offers an early view. In [Sandra's account of BugBasher](https://x.com/sandylikesfrogs/status/2100631407676829995), Devin received a payment card, phone, email, and Stripe link to build a pest-control referral business from restaurant inspection records.

A twenty-minute heartbeat checked progress. Scheduled agents called prospects, reviewed transcripts, and revised instructions; incoming calls and payments triggered immediate work. Shared files carried learning between sessions. Humans initially supervised closely and continued approving code changes.

Across 10,929 calls, the agent added language switching, struggled with speaking its internal instructions aloud, and escalated unresolved failures. It spent eight days waiting for a partner and discovered that unanswered human approvals were blocking progress. It eventually reached its owners by email.

It changed whom it contacted and how it sold: staffed commercial sales teams, a specific restaurant lead, then a $75 upfront payment. It mistook an hour without clicks for failure. A buyer paid eight hours later; delivery followed in about six minutes. The buyer wanted more leads, but the agent missed the repeat-sale opportunity.

Four weeks and roughly $3,000 of spending produced $75 in revenue. The experiment demonstrated adaptation and a completed sale; profitable, sustained operation remained unresolved.

For us, the lesson is that a business goal needs an operating process around it. The agent needs ways to observe what happened, carry learning forward, change its approach, and reach someone when a decision exceeds its authority. A schedule alone cannot supply those things.

Consider a future client onboarding operation. A person sets the desired completion rate, service standards, and budget. Opulent watches where clients stall, investigates the records, and coordinates the follow-up. It can change the sequence when the evidence supports a better approach. If a policy decision is blocking progress, it brings the owner a clear choice and keeps the remaining work moving.

Improvement would be measured across the whole responsibility. Faster replies are useful only if clients finish onboarding successfully. More activity is useful only if the result justifies the cost. A promising customer response should inform the next action, including returning to someone who has already bought.

This is the future we are building toward: Opulent maintains the state of the work, learns from results, and decides what to do next within the authority it has been given. People choose the priorities, resolve the consequential tradeoffs, and judge whether the operation is achieving its purpose.
